We are past the phase of debating the pros and cons of the digital technologies that dominate modern living. Whether we realize it or not, we (citizens, individuals, humans) are under attack. There's an assault on our selves, on our attention, on our minds.

We're at a point where a vast majority understands, consciously or not, that there's something insidious about social media and streaming platforms, into which so many of us sink our lives.

Like rats, we helplessly guzzle the sugary water pouring out of our screens, whether due to boredom, escapism, addiction or all three.

This assault on our minds masquerades as short-form videos, endless content from acquaintances we've collected over the years, award-winning series, "information", mediated social interactions and, more recently, sycophantic language simulators.

We're all both victims of and complicit in the assault, as it takes two to tango.

You must make the first move: pick up your phone, unlock it and open your app - Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Netflix, ✗) - whichever.

After that, you're under attack. You've entered a territory controlled by corporate entities that have demonstrated beyond doubt that they view humans as a commodity to exploit: meatbags whose lived experience and agency must be shaped and aligned with the private interests of the corporation.

In these dopamine slot machines we spend our minds. And, like in a casino, once you're inside your chances of losing go up.

In fact, it's so much more likely that you'll lose that the best strategy to win is never to step in.

The house always wins. It's rigged against you by design.

This means that our first line of defense for retaining control over our minds is to exit these places.

Not disengaging from technology as a whole. Not being against technology, as some would love to frame the argument.

Being conscious of when and how to use these technologies, re-learning to live without digital intermediaries and outright rejecting all attempts to subvert us.

It might sound counterintuitive.

Disengaging as a form of resistance.
